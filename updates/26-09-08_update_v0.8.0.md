## What was done

### Six zones, and the card editor — 2, 3

A card had a fixed shape with three dials aimed at it: where the avatar sat,
where the badges sat, where the dot sat. Nothing else could move, and the three
that could each needed their own setting, their own attribute and their own
block of stylesheet.

A card is now **six zones** — top left / top right, middle left / middle right,
bottom left / bottom right — and every part of it names one:

```
slots: { avatar:'tl', dot:'tr', name:'tl', desc:'ml',
         tag:'bl', badges:'br', count:'br', edit:'tr' }
```

That is the whole model. The parts are built once with the card and kept on it;
placing them is appending each into its zone in a fixed order, and it only
happens when the slots or the set of live parts actually changed — a card whose
layout has not moved is not touched, so nothing loses focus or restarts an
animation on a re-render.

A part that is **switched off is detached, not hidden**, which is what lets an
empty row stand down (`:has(> .zone > *)`) instead of leaving a gap where the
description used to be.

Each zone also has a **direction**: side by side, hung from the top, or stacked.
That last one is how the avatar gets above the name rather than beside it — what
used to be `avatarPos`, now a property of the place rather than of the picture.
`nameAlign` went the same way: it is the top-left zone hanging from the top.

The **card editor** is its own pane, opened by **card** on the bar or from
settings. At the top is a real card, built by `build()` and painted by `paint()`
inside a `.grid` of its own, so it inherits every rule the board's cards do and
there is no second renderer to keep in step. It stands in for a channel when the
board is empty and is the first channel on it otherwise. Underneath: the four
presets, the card's shape, a row per part (a switch, and a six-cell map of the
card drawn in the shape of the thing being edited), the six zone directions, and
every per-part dial.

The presets moved here and write slots and zones now, which is what makes
*poster* a different card rather than the same card with a bigger picture.

### Clearing the dots — 1

A board seeded in one pass — the **+ add** button over a subscriptions page adds
forty channels at once — arrives with a dot on every card, because every one of
them has posted since a `seen` it has never had.

**clear dots** appears on the bar only while there is something to clear, says
how many, and is two-step like every other clearing button on the board. A
single dot is cleared by clicking it: the dot is a button now, because it is the
control for the thing it means.

It stamps an acknowledgement and not a view. A new field, `dotAt`, sits beside
`seen`, and `isNew` compares the newest upload against the later of the two — so
the cards still say never opened, because they have not been.

### Posted today — 4

A dot says "there is something you have not seen". Whether it went up an hour
ago or in March is a different fact, and the one that decides what to open now,
so the **whole card** answers rather than a mark on it: the edge lifts to the
accent, a soft glow sits under it, the name takes the accent's own colour, and
the 3px colour spine runs a slow sheen down its length. The spine is the animated
part because it is 3px wide — a card that pulses is a card that shouts, and there
are forty of them. It stands down under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

It does not care whether the dot has been cleared. The window is a dial
(`freshHours`, 24 by default) and the whole effect is a switch.

### Ten colours of heat — 5

`t` is quantised before it is mixed: `round(t × (steps−1)) / (steps−1)`, ten
steps by default. A continuous ramp across forty cards is forty colours nobody
can tell apart, which is a texture and not a scale. The number of steps is a
setting, 2 to 20.

### Favourite categories — 6

A star on each row in the category manager. A favourite is pinned to the front of
every list you **pick** a category from — the filter chips, the quick menu on a
card's tag, the filing bar — and carries a small star on its chip. The order in
the manager, which is set by hand with the arrows, is left exactly as it is:
those are two different questions and only one of them is about what you use
every day. Pinning is a switch in settings.

### The filter's animation — 7

Two ways a board can change shape, and they wanted different animations. A
**reorder** — a different sort, a different size — is the same cards in new
boxes, so it is a FLIP. A **filter** is not that: turning on a category leaves a
board that mostly has nothing to do with the one before it, and FLIPping that
makes the survivors slide in from wherever they happened to have been, which is
the sliding this was asked to stop doing.

A chip click now sets a flag that skips the FLIP for that render and replays the
board's own entry instead — every card on the filtered board gets `.in` and its
own place in the stagger, exactly as when HUB is first opened. The class is
removed from all of them, one reflow is forced, and it goes back on, which is
what actually restarts a CSS animation on a node that already had it.

### A faster refresh — 8

Two changes, and the second is the one that matters most on a settled board.

**Lanes.** It was one channel at a time with a 350ms sleep between them, which on
forty channels is most of a minute spent asleep. It is now a small pool — five by
default, a dial from one to eight — with no sleep, each lane pulling the next job
off the pile as it finishes its own, so one slow channel holds up its own lane
and nothing else.

**Two clocks, not one.** A channel page is a megabyte of markup holding one thing
that ever changes; the feed is a few kilobytes and holds the only thing worth
polling. They are now on separate schedules: the feed every `checkEvery` hours,
the page only when the id or the avatar is missing, or once a fortnight
(`pageDays`). A new `pageAt` records the last page read. A refresh over a settled
board is forty small XML files instead of forty megabytes of HTML — and where the
id is already known, the two requests go out together instead of in sequence.

### The bug the screenshots found

Not asked for, but visible the moment the card editor was rendered: **every range
row in settings was 210px tall**. The row is built as `set-r set-<type>`, so a
range row carried the class `set-range` — which is also what the slider's own
wrapper was called, and `.set-range{flex:0 0 210px}` inside `#set-body`, a column
flex box, reads as a *height*. The wrapper is `set-slide` now. This has been
wrong since v0.7.0, when the settings pane started building itself from a list.

## Verification

`cd test && npm test` — 448 checks across the five files, all passing. The
harness gained coverage for every item above: moving a part between zones (and
the card in the editor moving with it), a part switched off leaving the card
entirely, zone directions, presets writing slots, the fresh card appearing and
its window being a dial, the heat landing on exact tenths, clearing every dot
without stamping a view, clearing one by clicking it, favourites pinning to the
front of the chips while the manager keeps its order, and every card on a
filtered board carrying the entry class and its stagger index.

jsdom does not lay out or paint, so the look was checked separately: headless
Chrome against a seeded twelve-channel board, screenshotted at the board, the
card editor (top and zones), the category manager, and all four presets. That is
what found the 210px rows. The spine's sheen is an animation and a still cannot
show it — that one needs Hugo's eyes.
